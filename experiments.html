01 🧊
Phase Change of Water (Ice → Water → Steam)
Observing three states and latent heat
Class 6, 9 Easy
▶

Aim

To observe the three states of water and understand phase changes by recording temperature vs time.

Materials Required

  • Ice cubes | Beaker | Bunsen burner / hot plate | Thermometer | Stopwatch

Procedure

1
Place ice cubes in the beaker and record the starting temperature (should be ≤ 0°C).
2
Start heating slowly and record temperature every 30 seconds. Observe: Temperature stays at 0°C while ice melts — this is Latent Heat of Fusion.
3
Continue heating after all ice melts. Temperature rises steadily through the liquid state.
4
At 100°C observe: temperature stays constant while water boils — this is Latent Heat of Vaporisation.
5
Note steam rising. Record final temperature and remove from heat. Allow to cool and observe condensation.

Observations

StageTemperatureStateParticle Behaviour
Initial≤ 0°CSolid (Ice)Vibrating in fixed positions
Melting0°C (constant)Solid → LiquidBreaking free from lattice
Liquid1–99°CLiquidSliding past each other
Boiling100°C (constant)Liquid → GasEscaping surface bonds
Steam>100°CGasRandom rapid movement

Conclusion

Water exists in three states. Phase changes occur at fixed temperatures (melting point = 0°C, boiling point = 100°C). During phase change, temperature remains constant as heat is used to break interparticle bonds (latent heat).

02 🟣
Diffusion of KMnO₄ in Water
Demonstrating diffusion and effect of temperature
Class 6, 9 Easy
▶

Aim

To demonstrate diffusion in liquids using KMnO₄ (potassium permanganate) crystals and study the effect of temperature on diffusion rate.

Materials

  • KMnO₄ crystals | 2 beakers | Cold water | Hot water | Stopwatch | Dropper

Procedure

1
Fill Beaker A with cold water and Beaker B with hot water (same volume).
2
Using a dropper, carefully place one drop of KMnO₄ solution in the centre of each beaker (do NOT stir).
3
Observe and time how long it takes for colour to spread completely in each beaker.
4
Record observations every 2 minutes for 10 minutes.

Observations

BeakerTemperatureTime for full diffusionRate
ACold (~10°C)~20–25 minutesSlow
BHot (~70°C)~3–5 minutesFast

Conclusion

Diffusion occurs in liquids due to continuous random motion of particles. Higher temperature → greater kinetic energy → faster diffusion. The KMnO₄ particles move from high concentration to low concentration spontaneously.

03 🔦
Tyndall Effect – Colloid vs Solution
Identifying colloids using laser light scattering
Class 9 Medium
▶

Aim

To distinguish between a true solution, colloid, and suspension using the Tyndall effect (light scattering).

Materials

  • Beakers | Salt solution (true solution) | Starch/milk solution (colloid) | Chalk water (suspension) | Laser pointer / strong torch | Dark room

Procedure

1
Prepare three beakers: (A) Salt dissolved in water, (B) Starch dissolved in water, (C) Chalk powder mixed in water.
2
In a dark room, shine a laser beam through each solution from the side.
3
Observe whether the beam path is visible (scattering) or invisible (no scattering) in each beaker.
4
Allow Beaker C to rest for 30 minutes and observe settling.

Observations

SampleTypeBeam Visible?Settles?
Salt waterTrue Solution❌ NoNo
Starch / MilkColloid✅ Yes (Tyndall)No
Chalk waterSuspension✅ Yes✅ Yes

Conclusion

Colloids show the Tyndall effect because their particles (1–100nm) scatter light. True solutions with particles <1nm do not scatter light. Suspensions also scatter light but particles settle out.

04 🏔️
Sublimation of Camphor / Naphthalene
Solid directly converting to gas
Class 6, 9 Easy
▶

Aim

To observe and understand the process of sublimation using camphor and to collect the sublimed vapour.

Materials

  • Camphor tablets | China dish | Glass funnel | Cotton | Bunsen burner | Tongs

Procedure

1
Place camphor tablets in the china dish on a wire gauze and tripod stand.
2
Invert a glass funnel over the china dish. Plug the top of the funnel with a cotton ball to prevent escape of vapours.
3
Heat gently with a spirit lamp or Bunsen burner.
4
Observe camphor disappearing WITHOUT melting first. White crystals form on the inner walls of the funnel (deposition).
5
Stop heating and let cool. Collect the white crystals — this is pure camphor (purification by sublimation!).

Conclusion

Camphor undergoes sublimation — it converts directly from solid to gas on heating. The gas then deposits back as solid on the cooler funnel walls. This property is used to purify camphor (and iodine, naphthalene).

05 🧂
Saturated & Unsaturated Solutions
Finding the solubility limit of salt in water
Class 6, 9 Easy
▶

Aim

To understand the concept of solubility by finding when a solution becomes saturated with salt (NaCl) at room temperature.

Procedure

1
Take 100 ml of water in a beaker at room temperature (~25°C).
2
Add 5g of NaCl and stir until dissolved completely — this is an unsaturated solution.
3
Continue adding 5g portions and stirring. Record when salt no longer dissolves.
4
When undissolved salt remains at bottom — solution is saturated.
5
Warm the solution and observe: saturated solution can dissolve more salt at higher temperature.

Observations

Salt AddedObservationState of Solution
5g, 10g, 15gDissolves completelyUnsaturated
~36gJust fully dissolvesSaturated (25°C)
40g+Salt remains undissolvedSaturated + excess

06 💨
Effect of Temperature on Rate of Diffusion in Gases
Comparing diffusion speeds at different temperatures
Class 9 Medium
▶

Aim

To show that gases diffuse faster at higher temperatures using an ammonia and HCl demonstration.

Materials

  • Long glass tube (30cm) | Cotton wool | Ammonia solution | HCl solution | Clamp stand | Ruler

Procedure

1
Soak one cotton plug in ammonia solution and another in concentrated HCl.
2
Simultaneously insert one plug at each end of the horizontal glass tube and seal lightly.
3
Observe where a white ring of NH₄Cl (ammonium chloride) forms inside the tube.
4
Measure the distance of the white ring from each end. Lighter NH₃ gas travels faster → ring forms closer to HCl end.

Conclusion

NH₃ (lighter gas, mol. mass = 17) diffuses faster than HCl (heavier gas, mol. mass = 36.5). This confirms Graham's Law: Rate of diffusion is inversely proportional to square root of molar mass.

07 🌡️
Effect of Pressure on Boiling Point
How reduced pressure lowers boiling point
Class 9 Advanced
▶

Aim

To demonstrate that the boiling point of water decreases when pressure is reduced (as happens at high altitudes).

Virtual Procedure (Simulator)

1
Open the Simulator and select Water (Paani) from the substance picker.
2
Set Temperature to 100°C — observe water boiling at standard pressure (P=0).
3
Decrease the Pressure slider. Observe the transition to gas state at lower temperatures.
4
Record the temperature at which water boils under reduced pressure.

Real-world Application

At high altitudes (e.g., Himalayan mountains), atmospheric pressure is lower, so water boils at <100°C. This is why cooking takes longer at high altitudes — food cooks at lower temperatures.

08 ☁️
Brownian Motion in Colloids
Observing random particle movement
Class 9 Medium
▶

Aim

To observe Brownian motion in a colloid and understand why colloidal particles do not settle.

Materials

  • Dilute milk solution (or starch solution) | Microscope | Slide | Cover slip | Strong light source

Procedure

1
Prepare a dilute milk solution (1 drop of milk in 100ml water) for better clarity.
2
Place a drop on a microscope slide, add cover slip carefully without air bubbles.
3
Observe under high magnification with strong side lighting.
4
Observe tiny fat globules moving in random, zigzag paths continuously.

Conclusion

The random zigzag motion of colloidal particles is called Brownian Motion. It is caused by unequal bombardment of colloidal particles by the surrounding smaller water molecules. This motion keeps colloid particles from settling.

09 🌊
Separation of Mixtures – Filtration & Evaporation
Separating salt from sand-water mixture
Class 6, 7 Easy
▶

Aim

To separate salt from a sand-salt mixture using filtration and evaporation techniques.

Procedure

1
Mix sand and salt together, then dissolve in water (salt dissolves, sand does not).
2
Filtration: Pour the mixture through filter paper in a funnel. Sand stays on filter paper; saltwater passes through.
3
Evaporation: Heat the filtered saltwater in an evaporating dish. Water evaporates; salt crystals remain.
4
Cool and collect the white salt crystals. Weigh to compare with original amount.

Conclusion

Filtration separates insoluble solid (sand) from liquid mixture. Evaporation separates soluble solid (salt) from its solution. These are physical separation methods that exploit differences in solubility and boiling point.

Latent Heat Demonstration
Why 0°C steam burns more than 0°C water
Class 9 Advanced
▶

Aim

To understand latent heat by comparing the cooling effect of melting ice vs cold water at the same temperature.

Virtual Procedure (Simulator)

1
Open Simulator, select Water, set Temperature to exactly 0°C. Observe: the Latent Heat Indicator flashes — system at melting point.
2
Observe that the AHA box shows: "Melting happening — temperature stays at 0°C even though heat is being added."
3
Note: Steam at 100°C has MORE energy than water at 100°C by the latent heat of vaporisation (2260 kJ/kg).
