States of Matter
Class 6

Matter exists in three main states — Solid, Liquid, and Gas. Each state has a definite particle arrangement:

  • Solid: Particles are tightly packed, vibrate in fixed positions. Definite shape and volume.
  • Liquid: Particles are close but can slide past each other. Definite volume, no fixed shape.
  • Gas: Particles are far apart, move randomly. No fixed shape or volume.
Key Concept

States of matter depend on the balance between interparticle attraction and kinetic energy of particles.

Evaporation
Class 6

Evaporation is the process where a liquid converts to gas (vapour) at temperatures below its boiling point, occurring only at the surface.

  • Factors that increase evaporation: Higher temperature, more surface area, wind/airflow, lower humidity.
  • Cooling effect: Evaporation absorbs latent heat from surroundings — e.g., sweating cools the body.
Key Formula

Rate of evaporation ∝ Temperature & Surface Area

Mixtures & Solutions
Class 6

A mixture is formed when two or more substances are mixed without a chemical reaction.

  • Homogeneous mixture: Uniform composition throughout (e.g., salt in water = solution).
  • Heterogeneous mixture: Non-uniform composition (e.g., sand in water = suspension).
  • Mixtures can be separated by filtration, evaporation, distillation, and chromatography.
Remember

In a solution: Solute + Solvent = Solution. Solute dissolves in the Solvent.

Physical & Chemical Changes
Class 7

Changes in matter are classified as physical or chemical:

  • Physical Change: No new substance formed. Reversible in most cases (e.g., melting ice, boiling water, tearing paper).
  • Chemical Change: New substance(s) formed. Usually irreversible (e.g., burning wood, rusting iron, cooking food).
Test for Chemical Change

Change in colour, smell, temperature, gas evolution, or precipitate formation.

Atoms & Molecules
Class 8

All matter is made of tiny particles called atoms. Atoms combine to form molecules.

  • Atom: Smallest unit of an element that retains its chemical properties.
  • Molecule: Two or more atoms bonded together (e.g., H₂O, CO₂, O₂).
  • Dalton's Atomic Theory: All matter is made of atoms; atoms of the same element are identical; atoms combine in fixed ratios.
Common Molecules

Water = H₂O | Salt = NaCl | Carbon dioxide = CO₂ | Oxygen = O₂

Matter in Our Surroundings
Class 9

This is the core chapter on particle nature of matter for Class 9:

  • Particles of matter are constantly moving — this is called the Kinetic Theory.
  • Latent Heat of Fusion: Heat needed to convert solid to liquid at melting point without temperature change.
  • Latent Heat of Vaporisation: Heat needed to convert liquid to gas at boiling point.
  • Sublimation: Solid directly converting to gas (e.g., camphor, iodine, dry ice).
  • Plasma: Fourth state — ionised gas at extremely high temperatures.
Phase Change Summary

Solid ⇌ Liquid (Melting/Freezing) | Liquid ⇌ Gas (Vaporisation/Condensation) | Solid ⇌ Gas (Sublimation)

Is Matter Around Us Pure?
Class 9

This chapter distinguishes pure substances from mixtures:

  • Pure Substance: Elements (e.g., Gold, Iron) and Compounds (e.g., Water, Salt) — fixed composition.
  • Solution: Homogeneous mixture. Solute particles are invisible (<1nm). Does NOT scatter light.
  • Suspension: Heterogeneous, particles settle on standing (>100nm). E.g., chalk in water.
  • Colloid: Heterogeneous, particles don't settle (1–100nm). Scatters light — Tyndall Effect.
Tyndall Effect

The scattering of light by colloidal particles. Visible in: milk, smoke, fog, starch solution.

Diffusion
Class 9

Diffusion is the movement of particles from a region of high concentration to low concentration.

  • Diffusion occurs in solids, liquids, and gases.
  • Gases diffuse fastest (high kinetic energy, large intermolecular spaces).
  • Solids diffuse slowest (tightly packed particles, low energy).
  • Diffusion rate increases with temperature.
  • Examples: Smell of perfume spreading, KMnO₄ in water, gas leaks.
Fick's Law (simplified)

Rate of diffusion ∝ Concentration Gradient & Temperature

Chemical Reactions & Equations
Class 10

A chemical reaction is a process where reactants are converted to products with different properties.

  • Combination: A + B → AB (e.g., 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O)
  • Decomposition: AB → A + B (e.g., 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂)
  • Displacement: A + BC → AC + B
  • Double Displacement: AB + CD → AD + CB
  • Redox: Simultaneous oxidation and reduction
Law of Conservation of Mass

Mass of reactants = Mass of products. Atoms are neither created nor destroyed.

Acids, Bases & Salts
Class 10

Understanding acids, bases, and salts is fundamental to chemistry:

  • Acid: pH < 7, tastes sour, turns blue litmus red. Examples: HCl, H₂SO₄, CH₃COOH
  • Base: pH > 7, tastes bitter, soapy feel, turns red litmus blue. Examples: NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)₂
  • Neutral: pH = 7 (water)
  • Salt: Formed by acid + base neutralisation. Examples: NaCl, Na₂SO₄
pH Scale

0 (very acidic) ← 7 (neutral) → 14 (very basic)

Metals & Non-metals
Class 10

Physical and chemical properties distinguish metals from non-metals:

  • Metals: Lustrous, malleable, ductile, good conductors. E.g., Fe, Cu, Au, Al
  • Non-metals: Brittle, non-lustrous, poor conductors. E.g., C, S, N, O
  • Reactivity Series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Cu > Ag > Au
  • Corrosion: Slow destruction of metals by environmental reactions (rusting of Fe).
Ionic Bonding

Metals lose electrons (form cations) + Non-metals gain electrons (form anions) → Ionic compound

📖 Key Terms Glossary

Quick reference for important chemistry vocabulary.

Sublimation
Direct conversion of solid to gas without passing through liquid state. E.g., Camphor, Dry Ice.
Latent Heat
Heat energy absorbed or released during a phase change at constant temperature.
Diffusion
Spontaneous movement of particles from high concentration to low concentration region.
Tyndall Effect
Scattering of a light beam by colloidal particles, making the path of light visible.
Colloid
A mixture with particles (1–100nm) that don't settle but scatter light. E.g., milk, fog.
Suspension
Heterogeneous mixture with particles >100nm that settle on standing. E.g., chalk in water.
Brownian Motion
Random zigzag movement of colloidal particles due to unequal bombardment by fluid molecules.
Boiling Point
Temperature at which a liquid converts to gas (vapour pressure = atmospheric pressure).
Melting Point
Temperature at which a solid converts to liquid at standard pressure.
Kinetic Energy
Energy of motion. Particles in gas state have highest KE; solids have lowest KE.
Solubility
Maximum amount of solute that dissolves in a given solvent at a given temperature.
Plasma
The fourth state of matter — super-heated ionised gas. E.g., stars, lightning, neon signs.
